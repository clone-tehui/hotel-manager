# ChiHome serviced-apartment revenue-management knowledge

This is retrieval knowledge, not a rigid rule engine. Apply only when supported by current verified data.

## Core business framing
- Inventory is perishable: an unbooked apartment-night cannot be sold after the date passes.
- Optimize total room contribution, not occupancy alone. Revenue, achieved nightly rate, monthly operating cost, remaining sellable nights and channel cost all matter.
- Current-month and next-month are distinct commercial problems. Never use current-month break-even as the governing objective for next-month strategy.
- Weeks crossing a month boundary must be evaluated per calendar-month segment for revenue/cost reasoning.

## Booking-first lifecycle per room and assessed period
1. No booking signal: objective is the first qualified booking. Form a measurable hypothesis (dates, stay length, audience, channel, offer), run a short test and preserve a baseline.
2. Weak signal after campaign: compare booked nights, achieved rate and revenue against baseline. Change one major variable at a time so learning is attributable.
3. Positive signal below target: scale/refine what worked; do not discard a winner merely because break-even has not yet arrived.
4. Meaningful booked-night base but not break-even in the **same assessed month**: recover price and target the remaining revenue gap without killing conversion.
5. Break-even achieved in the **same assessed month**: monetize remaining vacant nights for incremental profit, respecting price floor, channel cost and displacement risk.

## Period-specific mindset
- This week: urgency, orphan gaps, arrival friction and last-minute conversion. Protect already-strong nights.
- Next week: length-of-stay bundles, channel/audience targeting and early intervention before last-minute discounting.
- This month: booked-night pace plus assessed-month revenue versus assessed-month operating cost.
- Next month (opened on day 15): build early base demand, compare pickup/pace, avoid premature deep discount, and seek longer-stay/corporate demand where appropriate. Break-even belongs to next month, not current month.

## Evidence dimensions the CEO should consider
- Booked nights / sellable nights and contiguous vacancy pattern.
- Booking pickup since last analysis/campaign, lead time and cancellation risk.
- Achieved nightly rate and room-month revenue allocated only to nights in that month.
- Monthly operating cost and remaining revenue gap for the assessed month.
- Room/building/type, current reference price, discountable price/price floor if available.
- Historical same-room response, active campaign, review due date and prior failed/winning hypotheses.
- Source/channel mix and channel cost when those facts exist; never invent OTA availability or commission.

## Experiment discipline
- Every campaign needs baseline, objective, date range, audience/channel hypothesis, offer, success criteria, review date and fallback.
- Review after a default 3 days unless the stay date is too near/far; the model may justify another window.
- Success is not simply “some booking”: measure incremental booked nights, revenue, achieved rate and progress toward the phase objective.
- Avoid overlapping contradictory campaigns for the same room/date/channel.
- Keep recommendation advisory until an approved external-action tool exists and returns success.

## Token discipline
- Use compact per-room/month facts; exclude guest PII and free-text notes.
- Cohort similar rooms dynamically by evidence and history, reason once per cohort, then emit only room-specific differences.
- Retrieve only relevant knowledge sections and recent/high-importance memories; summarize old runs rather than replaying them.
